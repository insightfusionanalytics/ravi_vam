[15/05/26, 7:51:39 AM] ~ Ravi mareedu: To create a synthetic formula for the 3x leveraged S&P 500 ETF (UPRO), we can use a linear regression model or a cost-of-carry derivation. Because UPRO seeks to provide $3 \times$ the daily return of the S&P 500 (SPX), its price action is driven by the underlying index minus the costs of leverage (interest rates) and management fees.

### 1. The Daily Return Formula

The most accurate way to model the daily price movement of UPRO is:

$$R_{UPRO} = 3(R_{SPX}) - [2(f) + ER] \cdot \frac{1}{252}$$

**Where:**

* $R_{UPRO}$: The daily percentage return of UPRO.
* $R_{SPX}$: The daily percentage return of the S&P 500 Index.
* $f$: The current funding rate (typically Fed Funds Rate + a small spread, currently around 5.3%–5.5%).
* $ER$: The Expense Ratio (0.91% for UPRO).
* $252$: The number of trading days in a year.

---

### 2. The Multi-Day Price Formula (Geometric)

To calculate a "synthetic" price over time ($P_t$), you must account for **volatility decay** (slippage). The formula for the price at time $t$ relative to the starting price $P_0$ is:

$$P_t = P_0 \cdot \left( \frac{SPX_t}{SPX_0} \right)^3 \cdot e^{-3(3-1) \frac{\sigma^2}{2} t}$$

**Variables Explained:**

* **The Leverage Multiplier:** $(\frac{SPX_t}{SPX_0})^3$ represents the idealized triple return.
* **The Decay Constant:** $e^{-3\sigma^2 t}$ (where $\sigma$ is the daily volatility). This is the "hidden tax" of daily rebalancing. In high-volatility markets, this causes the synthetic price to drift lower than the expected $3 \times$ return.

---

### 3. Simplified Backtesting Approximation

If you are building a spreadsheet for historical backtesting and don't want to calculate daily funding rates, you can use this simplified linear approximation:

$$UPRO_{synthetic} = (3 \times \text{Daily \% Change SPX}) - 0.00045$$

> **Note:** The $-0.00045$ (4.5 basis points) is a rough daily "drag" estimate that accounts for a 5% interest rate environment plus the expense ratio. This will vary significantly based on the current yield curve.

### Key Considerations

* **Tracking Error:** Synthetic formulas often underperform the actual ETF during periods of extreme market stress due to swap counterparty costs that aren't captured in simple math.
* **Dividends:** The S&P 500 price index does not include dividends. For a truly accurate synthetic, you should use the **S&P 500 Total Return Index (SPXTR)** as your base.
